Two CUROP Summer Studentships in COMSC available

The Cardiff Undergraduate Research Opportunities Programme (CUROP) programme provides summer placements for Cardiff University undergraduates in the University research environment.

Two CUROP studentships in the area of visual computing are available in the School on projects supervised by Prof Paul Rosin & Dr Yukun Lai.

CUROP offers a stipend of £1600 (£200 per week) to support a student on a placement of eight weeks duration. It is now considered to be one of the largest undergraduate research schemes in the UK. It offers excellent work experience, experience of working on a research project and looks excellent on any student's CV!

1: Artistic Rendering of RGB-D Images

Artistic rendering techniques are computational methods that generate images mimicking styles of artists. Such techniques form a central set of features for photo processing software such as Adobe Photoshop, in the form of a rich set of filters. Traditional artistic rendering usually takes an image as input and generates a stylised image. However, since images lose 3D spatial information, artistic rendering techniques can easily make mistakes in interpreting image content, e.g. losing essential lines that separate adjacent objects with similar colour, or failing to draw foreground with more details than background.

In recent years, RGB-D (colour + depth) data becomes popular, due to the availability of low-cost devices such as Kinect. Even with traditional images, depth information can be reasonably estimated using latest deep learning based techniques. This project will improve image-based artistic rendering techniques the supervisors have developed by exploiting additional depth cues, including enhanced object separation, adaptive level of details, and enhanced rendering elements guided by depth.

The student will be exposed to a variety of topics in visual computing such as 3D analysis, image rendering, and will benefit from the existing artistic rendering code developed by the supervisors. The student will improve their programming skills, develop research/problem solving skills, and communication skills.

2: Measuring 3D Mesh Saliency using an Eye Tracker

In recent years 3D data has become popular as low-cost devices such as Kinect have emerged. There are many applications of 3D data, such as gaming, cinematography special effects, 3D printing, etc. When these models are manipulated (eg downsized, stretched, morphed, etc) then important (ie salient) features should be preserved as best as possible.

This project will develop a method for measuring the salience of parts of the 3D mesh data Experiments will be carried out with an eye tracker to provide an indication of saliency that will be consistent with human judgement Existing models are based on low level geometric features extracted from the data Using machine learning and the eye tracking data, the student will optimise such a model to better predict human perception of salience.

The student will be exposed to a variety of topics in visual computing such as 3D rendering, 3D modelling, and will have access to the School's state-of-the-art eye tracker. The student will improve their programming skills, data analysis skills, and gain experience in setting up and conducting user studies, as well as develop problem solving skills for carrying out research.
